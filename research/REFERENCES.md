# References

Checked sources for the claims in [`01_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md`](01_EVIDENCE_AND_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md) and [`02_SHARED_LANGUAGE_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md`](02_SHARED_LANGUAGE_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md).

The synthesis was first written from four unpublished research reports produced with a deep-research tool (see [`docs/provenance.md`](../docs/provenance.md)). On 22 September 2026 every source the public text relies on was looked up again at the publisher or in an index record. Every cited work exists, and title, year, venue and DOI match what is given here. Where a field could not be confirmed (some author lists, one year, some volume and page numbers, one study's sample size), the entry says "not confirmed" and why. Numbers stated in the synthesis were checked against the paper where the entry says so. The check found one wrong author list in the unpublished reports (Bansal et al. 2021, corrected here) and no mismatched numbers.

**How to read the tags.** *peer-reviewed*, *preprint* or *practitioner* (books and method literature, not controlled evidence). The note after each entry says how it was checked — **C**: publisher metadata retrieved; **S**: found at the publisher or an index whose record matches, DOI confirmed where the publisher URL carries it — and names any field that could not be confirmed.

**What this check did not do.** It did not re-read the papers in full, and it did not run a new literature search. It establishes that the cited work exists and says what the synthesis says it says at the level of its abstract. Where that is weaker than the synthesis's wording, the section [Where the support is thinner than the wording](#where-the-support-is-thinner-than-the-wording) says so.

## Where the support is thinner than the wording

The citation check found claims in `research/01` §2 — the section headed "strongly supported" — and in `research/02` whose support is narrower than their wording. They are listed here rather than silently reworded, and recorded as known finding [KF-3](../docs/known-findings.md).

| Claim | What the sources actually support |
|---|---|
| 01 §2 *Language and model can co-evolve* | Practitioner method literature only (Evans 2003). No empirical study. |
| 01 §2 *AI can be highly valuable for articulation, search, synthesis and critique* | Articulation and drafting (one requirements study; Noy & Zhang 2023). Nothing in the evidence covers search or critique; synthesis rests on one preprint ([SalgadoNeto2026](#salgadoneto2026)), which the synthesis does not cite. |
| 01 §2 *Human situated input remains epistemically special* | Requirements are co-constructed and partly tacit (Ferrari et al.). "Epistemically special" is the project's normative reading, not a finding. |
| 01 §2 *Shared terminology reduces coordination cost* | Shown in laboratory referential tasks (Brennan & Clark 1996). Not measured in discovery or software work. |
| 01 §2 *Selective cognitive engagement matters* | Buçinca et al. tested cognitive forcing, not selective forcing. "Selective" is inference. |
| 01 §3 contributions as epistemic/normative roles | Interpretation, consistent with Vaccaro et al. 2024; no study tests it. |
| 01 §4 *Probe ≠ Candidate ≠ Recommendation ≠ Decision ≠ Commitment* | A protocol construct built on co-evolution, parallel prototyping and fixation research; the construct itself is untested. |
| 01 §5 *no full glossary is justified* | Absence of evidence for a glossary, not evidence against one. |
| 01 §5 *scenario/boundary use can test meaning* | Practitioner literature only. |
| 02 *labels can influence categorization and attention* | Categorization: supported (Lupyan et al. 2007; Gervits et al. 2023), with task-dependent effects. Attention/perception rests on work whose early effects did not replicate ([NonReplication2021](#nonreplication2021)). |
| 02 *LLM reformulation can reveal implicit content* | One study, in structured requirements writing rather than open discovery. |
| 02 *candidate terms as semantic probes*; the failure-mode list | Protocol inference, not findings. |

Two smaller points: `01` §2A says Vaccaro et al. are cited in §2 — they now are, through this file. And the statement that the meta-analysis "predates most current models" has not been checked against its inclusion window. For Shin et al. 2025, the sample size (N=280) is confirmed in the abstract, and the four conditions in the full text (three LLM approaches plus a manual baseline).
