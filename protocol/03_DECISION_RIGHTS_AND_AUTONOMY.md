# Decision Rights & Adaptive Autonomy v0.2.1

> **Status:** experimental, protocol v0.2.1. Rules may still change between versions.

## 1. Principle

> **Initiative is a property of the next move, not a permanent role.**

The protocol is intentionally asymmetric.

AI may take extensive initiative where work is:

- externally searchable,
- analytical,
- reversible,
- verifiable,
- low-consequence.

Human cognition becomes more important as work becomes:

- locally situated,
- normative,
- consequential,
- irreversible,
- difficult to verify,
- framing-bearing.

---

## 2. Five routing dimensions

| Dimension | More AI initiative | More human engagement |
|---|---|---|
| Epistemic locality | public / searchable | only locally known |
| Normativity | descriptive / analytical | what should matter / count |
| Consequence | local downstream effect | path-dependent |
| Reversibility | cheap to undo | expensive to undo |
| Verifiability | objective / fast | subjective / hard |

This heuristic remains experimental as an exact router.

---

## 3. AI-owned by default

Normally no approval needed for:

- state maintenance;
- formatting;
- deduplication;
- clustering;
- traceability;
- terminology consistency checks;
- diffing;
- collecting open questions;
- external factual research;
- reversible draft updates;
- mechanical validation.

"AI does it and asks approval for everything" is not meaningful externalization.

---

## 4. AI-heavy, human-steered

AI can execute most of:

- research;
- evidence synthesis;
- alternative frames;
- analogies;
- failure modes;
- counterarguments;
- hypothesis generation;
- consequence analysis;
- model variants;
- candidate terminology.

Human steers relevance and rejects inappropriate framing.

---

## 5. Shared construction

Normally co-developed:

- problem/opportunity frame;
- conceptual solution;
- domain/information model;
- requirements;
- success criteria;
- design principles;
- important shared terminology;
- validation strategy.

AI may draft aggressively, but meaning-bearing changes must remain corrigible.

---

## 6. AI advisory only

AI should advise rather than silently decide:

- problem-frame selection;
- priority;
- scope cuts;
- risk acceptance;
- value trade-offs;
- major candidate selection;
- user-visible product meaning;
- canonicalization of framing-bearing concepts.

Good advice includes:
- alternatives;
- criteria;
- evidence;
- counterargument;
- uncertainty;
- reversibility;
- model implications.

---

## 7. Human-owned commitments

Explicit human ownership remains for:

- product vision;
- accepted working frame for current horizon;
- scope/non-goals;
- priorities;
- accepted product risk;
- consequential trade-offs;
- high-impact terminology commitments where meaning affects the model;
- final discovery baseline.

---

## 8. Friction proportional to consequence

### Low consequence
AI proceeds autonomously.

### Medium consequence
Evidence + alternatives + human judgment.

### High consequence
Context + assumptions + independent human position where useful + AI challenge + explicit commitment.

Do not use high-friction patterns everywhere.

---

## 9. Shared-language autonomy rule

AI is free to suggest terminology.

But for **model-relevant language**:

- naming is a proposal;
- interpretation must remain distinguishable from the word chosen;
- unresolved consequential ambiguity should be surfaced;
- canonicalization is a small model commitment;
- repeated use is not enough to infer agreement.

---

## 10. Failure patterns

### Rubber-stamping
Large decision bundle → "go?"

### Approval theater
Human reviews mechanical work.

### Silent commitment
Recommendation or working term becomes baseline through inaction.

### AI-first framing lock
Polished framing removes useful human alternatives.

### False shared understanding
Same word, different conceptual boundaries.

### False precision
A crisp label or definition hides unresolved meaning.

### Vocabulary bureaucracy
Every phrase becomes managed terminology.

### Hidden value judgment
Normative choice presented as analysis.

### Over-gating
Too many interruptions train passive approval.

### Human retrieval waste
Human is asked to retrieve what AI could research.

### Analogy capture
A prior framework imports hidden assumptions because it looks similar.
